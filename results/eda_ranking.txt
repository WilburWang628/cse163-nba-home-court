1. DATA SIZE
Raw ranking.csv: 210342 rows x 13 columns
ranking_clean.csv: 197350 rows x 18 columns
Raw teams.csv: 30 rows x 14 columns
Each row of ranking.csv is one team's standings on one day (wins, losses, home record and road record so far that season). The file covers 7023 different dates, which is why it has so many rows: about 30 teams x hundreds of days per season.
ranking_clean.csv keeps only regular-season standings from SEASON 2003 on, drops repeated team/season/date rows and adds SEASON, HOME_W, HOME_L, ROAD_W and ROAD_L.
Each row of teams.csv is one current NBA franchise (30 teams).

6. RESULT VALIDITY - NULL HYPOTHESIS A
H0: all 30 teams have the same mean HCA. H1: at least one team's mean HCA is different.
Planned test: one-way ANOVA (scipy.stats.f_oneway), 30 groups of 16 seasons each.
  F = 1.539, p = 0.0381 -> reject H0 at alpha = 0.05
  Effect size eta^2 = 0.090 (share of HCA variation explained by which team it is)
  Context: with about 41 home and 41 road games a season, chance alone gives one season's HCA an SD of about 0.103. The observed SD of HCA around each team's own mean is 0.105, so most of the season-to-season spread is game-to-game luck, and team differences are small next to it.

Assumption 1 - equal variances across teams (Levene's test, median-centered):
  W = 1.256, p = 0.1718. Team SDs of HCA range from 0.075 to 0.157.
  -> MET
Assumption 2 - roughly normal values in each group:
  Shapiro-Wilk per team: 0 of 30 teams have p < 0.05 (about 1.5 would be expected by chance even if all were normal). With only 16 values per team these tests have little power, so we also test the pooled residuals.
  Shapiro-Wilk on all 480 residuals (HCA minus team mean): W = 0.9956, p = 0.1895. Q-Q plot: figures/fig13_hca_residual_qq.png
  -> MET
Assumption 3 - independent observations:
  Different teams are different groups, but the same team's seasons may be linked (same coach, players, arena).
  Lag-1 correlation of a team's residual with its next season's residual: r = -0.068. Subtracting the team mean by itself pushes this correlation to about -1/(16 - 1) = -0.067 even for fully independent seasons, so the observed value is what independence predicts.
  -> MET
  Caveat: all teams share league-wide changes in a season (e.g. 2021 has the lowest league HCA). That affects every team the same way, so it adds noise but does not favor any team.

Backup if the assumptions fail: Kruskal-Wallis test (scipy.stats.kruskal), which compares ranks and does not need normality.
  H = 36.671, p = 0.1548 -> fail to reject H0

7. RESULT VALIDITY - NULL HYPOTHESIS B
H0: the mean HCA of Denver and Utah equals the mean HCA of the other 28 teams. H1: the means differ.
Altitude group: n = 32 team-seasons, mean HCA = 0.240, SD = 0.142
Other teams:    n = 448 team-seasons, mean HCA = 0.177, SD = 0.107
Difference in means: +0.063
Ranks of the team means (1 = largest HCA of 30): Denver Nuggets: 4, Utah Jazz: 1

Planned test: Welch's t-test (scipy.stats.ttest_ind, equal_var=False).
  t = 2.467, two-sided p = 0.018884 -> reject H0
  One-sided p (altitude HCA greater) = 0.009442
  For comparison, Student's t-test (equal variances): p = 0.001671
Why Welch: the groups are very unequal in size (32 vs 448). Student's t-test assumes equal variances, and when the small group has a different variance its p-value can be badly wrong. Welch's test does not assume equal variances and loses almost nothing when they are equal.

Assumption 1 - normality: the t-test compares means, and with n = 32 and 448 the sample means are close to normal (central limit theorem).
  Shapiro-Wilk on the altitude group: p = 0.3477 -> MET
Assumption 2 - equal variances: NOT needed for Welch. (Levene's test between the two groups: p = 0.0082.)
Assumption 3 - independence: the 32 altitude team-seasons come from only 2 franchises, so they are not 32 independent draws if a team's HCA is partly a stable team trait. Team-level check that treats each team as one observation: an exact permutation test over all 435 pairs of teams. If altitude did not matter, Denver + Utah would be just a random pair.
  Share of pairs with an average mean HCA >= Denver + Utah's: p = 0.0069 (one-sided) -> reject H0
  (A t-test on the 30 team means is not used: with only 2 teams in one group, their variance estimate is unreliable.)

Backup if the assumptions fail: Mann-Whitney U test (two-group version of Kruskal-Wallis).
  p = 0.014520 -> reject H0
