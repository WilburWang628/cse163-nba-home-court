1. RQ1 B - logistic regression (one row per game)
Model: logit P(home win) ~ SEASON, normal regular-season games (n = 19439).
Why: OLS on 16 season averages throws away the game-level data and gives the short 2011 season (990 games) the same weight as the others. Logistic regression uses every game and models a 0/1 outcome directly.
All normal seasons: slope = -0.00999 log-odds per season, p = 0.000865, odds ratio per decade = 0.905; predicted home win rate 0.609 (first season) -> 0.568 (last season)
Without SEASON 2021: slope = -0.00647 log-odds per season, p = 0.06227, odds ratio per decade = 0.937; predicted home win rate 0.605 (first season) -> 0.583 (last season)
Linearity check (likelihood-ratio test, straight line vs one effect per season): LR = 10.012, df = 14, p = 0.7613. No evidence against a straight line.
Assumption: games are independent (same caveat as validity_checks.txt).

2. Playoff dependence - series-level permutation tests
Problem: playoff games come in series of 4-7 games between the same two teams, so games in a series are not independent. The game-level tests treat 83 bubble playoff games as 83 independent observations, but they come from only 15 series.
Fix: shuffle whole series between periods instead of single games. Exchangeability then only needs different series to be independent. 10000 shuffles, seed 163; the statistic is the per-game mean (win rate or POINT_DIFF) in the period minus the normal period.
    period        outcome  observed_diff  games  series  p_game_level  p_series_level
covid_2021 HOME_TEAM_WINS       -0.07437     85      15        0.2118           0.163
covid_2021     POINT_DIFF         -1.165     85      15        0.4506          0.4272
    bubble HOME_TEAM_WINS        -0.1571     83      15        0.0044          0.0046
    bubble     POINT_DIFF         -3.506     83      15         0.022          0.0194
If p_series_level is much larger than p_game_level, the game-level result was overstated by treating games in a series as independent.

Why the two p-values are close: the variance of series-level home win rates is 0.90 times what independent games would give (1 = independent). Home court alternates between the two teams in a series, so the stronger team's edge shows up as home wins in some games and home losses in others, and HOME_TEAM_WINS is close to uncorrelated within a series.

4. Power analysis (alpha = 0.05, two-sided, normal period as the comparison group)
power: chance the test would detect a true difference as large as the observed one. min_detectable: smallest true difference (win-rate points or POINT_DIFF points) detected with 80% power. games_needed: period games needed to detect the observed difference with 80% power.
Power computed from the observed difference is only a rough guide; min_detectable is the more useful number because it does not depend on this sample's result.
